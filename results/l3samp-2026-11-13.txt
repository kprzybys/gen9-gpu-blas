## Reading
- Sampler alone: 100-104 GB/s from L3 (half the data port's 200), 63-65 GB/s for a 1 MB image.
- The paths add up only partly: data-port + sampler peak at 241 GB/s with 2 block reads per sampler read (+20 % over
  the port alone), 215 GB/s at 3:1 to 6:1 (+8 %), against 301 / 268 / 233 if fully additive. Same at 64 KB and
  256 KB, so it is not the sampler's own cache.
- Beyond L3 (1 MB) the sampler slows everything: mixes give 91-125 GB/s, below the port alone (194).
- Verdict: a second read path worth at most ~+20 % of L3 bandwidth, only for L3-resident data and only for kernels
  that are bound by the data port. None of today's hot kernels is (GEMM and Qwen attention are issue-bound,
  results/l3port-2026-11-13.txt). Not pursued. Untested and possibly more useful: the sampler's free format
  conversion (UNORM_INT8 / HALF -> float) as a way to remove decode instructions from issue-bound loops.
