## Reading
- The asm kernels are not stall-limited: EuStall 3 % (Qwen) / 3.4 % (Llama). When an EU is active, both FPU
  pipes are busy 86-88 % of the time (BothFPU / EuActive). The remaining gap to the peak is EU time WITHOUT work:
  23 % (Qwen) / 13 % (Llama) idle, with thread occupancy 70 % / 80 %.
- This contradicts the stage 2b explanation "Qwen sits on the L3 read bandwidth": L3 shader throughput is 112 GB/s
  (the kernel's own estimate was ~148) and stalls are 3 %. [HYP] The idle time comes from work distribution:
  thread dispatch granularity (a 128-item work-group = 16 SIMD8 threads; 56 thread slots per subslice hold 3
  work-groups = 48 threads = 86 % at most) and the tail of the grid. local 24 (3 threads per group) is 3.5 %
  faster with less idle time (17.5 %), consistent with this, though stalls rise to 8 %.
- The OpenCL C variant A: more idle (27 %) and more stall (9 %), BothFPU only 39.5 % -> its loss is mostly in the
  instruction stream (fewer dual-issued mads), as stage 2 concluded, plus the same idle share.
- Llama (4 rows per subgroup, more work per thread) has the highest occupancy and least idle time.
- GTI read throughput (memory side of L3) varies 0.8-12.9 GB/s between runs of similar kernels; not interpreted
  until its units/semantics are checked (it may count only part of the traffic).
Next lever for Qwen, by these counters: the ~20 % idle EU time (work-group size / grid shape / persistent threads),
not memory.
